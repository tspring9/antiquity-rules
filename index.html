Antiquity

Rules of Play

A game of empire in the ancient Mediterranean. Raise armies, march them across provinces, ship them over the sea, and win battles decided by twelve-sided dice and the width of the battle line.

Each power has a one-page faction sheet with its capital and build options, and each scenario has a setup sheet with its starting map and armies.

1Introduction

Antiquity is played by one to seven players. Each player leads one of the great powers of the ancient world — Rome, Carthage, the Hellenistic kingdoms, Persia and others — while barbarian tribes hold the edges of the map.

Every game is played from a scenario. Scenarios range from a solo game, holding a vast empire together against revolt, to open wars between five or seven rival powers. Each scenario sheet sets out:

  • which powers are in play, and the turn order
  • who starts where, with which units and capitals
  • which provinces raise barbarians
  • any special events, invasions or enemies
  • how the game is won

This book covers the rules common to every scenario.

The golden rule

Where a scenario sheet contradicts this book, the scenario sheet wins.

2Components

  • Map board — land territories and sea zones.
  • Unit pieces in each power's colour, plus markers for wounded War Elephants.
  • Twelve-sided dice (d12) — at least eight. Two colours help: one for the attacker, one for the defender.
  • Control markers to show who owns each territory, and capital markers.
  • Stronghold markers, and numbered siege markers (1–4).
  • A gold tracker for each player.
  • A battle mat laid out as in §9.1.
  • A scenario sheet.

3The Map

3.1Territories and sea zones

The board is divided into land territories and sea zones. Land units stand on land, and ships sail at sea.

Two spaces are adjacent if they share a border. Narrow straits count as a border: Messina, the Bosporus, Gibraltar, Bonifacio and Hormuz.

3.2Ownership

Every land territory is owned by one power. You keep a territory until an enemy takes it, even if you leave no units there. Sea zones are never owned.

3.3Income

Each territory you own pays 1 gold at the start of your turn. A capital pays 2 gold. Sea zones pay nothing, and neither does a surrounded stronghold (§11.3).

3.4Capitals

Each power has a capital — its seat of power. Most scenarios use the usual seat (Roma for Rome, Carthage for Carthage, Alexandria for Ptolemaic Egypt, and so on), but a scenario may name a different one. Capitals are often victory objectives.

3.5Strongholds

A stronghold is a territory with a walled city. Strongholds are usually capitals and major cities, but only the territories named on the scenario sheet count. A stronghold can hold off an army for several turns (§11).

4Dice

Every roll in Antiquity is a single d12. Each unit has a target number. Roll the target or lower to score a hit.

  • A unit that is attacking uses its Attack, plus any modifiers.
  • A unit that is defending uses its Defense. The only thing that ever modifies Defense is the Hoplites' Phalanx (§5).

Defense does not make a unit harder to kill. Every unit except the War Elephant dies to a single hit, attacking or defending. Defense is how hard a unit strikes back when it is attacked.

Levy

3/3 · move 1 · cost 2

Cheap troops who fill out the battle line. A Levy counts toward a full line (which matters for Hoplites), but it cannot pacify a province and cannot fight as a marine.

Heavy Infantry

4/4 · move 1 · cost 3

The backbone of an army. Heavy Infantry, the Legion and the Hoplite are the three heavy infantry units. They share the same stats, and each power can raise only one of them (§6). Every heavy infantry unit:

  • calms an occupied barbarian province (§14.2)
  • fights as a marine when carried on an Attack Boat (§12.5)

Heavy Infantry itself has no special rule.

Legion

4/4 · move 1 · cost 3

Manipular. Legions attack at +1 while their side's infantry line is not full. The legion wins the grind: it gets stronger as the battle thins the ranks. It is also the best unit for storming a city. An attacker's line at a stronghold has only four slots, so it is never full, and Legions attack at +1 for the whole assault (§11.4).

Hoplite

4/4 · move 1 · cost 3

Phalanx. Hoplites get +1 to both Attack and Defense while their side's infantry line is full, with all five front slots occupied. Levies count toward a full line. The phalanx wins the opening clash in the open field, and a full phalanx behind city walls is the hardest garrison to crack. It is no use for storming a city: an attacker's four-slot line at a stronghold is never full, so attacking Hoplites never get Phalanx there (§11.4). The line is checked at the start of each round (§9.3), so the Hoplites keep their bonus for the round in which the line breaks, and lose it from the following round.

Cavalry

4/3 · move 2 · cost 4

Moves 2 with no force-march penalty. Only one horseman fits in the front line. A second is not wasted: it is the only unit that can answer a flank attack (§10).

Horse Archer

4/4 · move 2 · cost 5
Parthia only

Mounted bowmen who shoot, wheel away and shoot again. At Carrhae in 53 BC they destroyed Crassus's legions.

Limited. Parthia may have at most two Horse Archers on the board. If one is killed, Parthia may buy another.

Own lane. A Horse Archer fights from the one-slot Horse Archer lane in the main wing, or from the Flank Horse Archer post in a flanking battle (§9.1). The lane sits outside the normal battle width. No enemy lane faces it, so hits reach it only by spillover, after every other lane except the General (§9.4).

Command. Gets +1 from a General in the same wing, like Cavalry.

Not in assaults. There is no Horse Archer lane when Parthia assaults a stronghold. Its Horse Archers fight in the Cavalry lane as ordinary Cavalry (§11.4). A Parthian garrison defending a stronghold keeps the lane.

Moves 2 with no force-march penalty.

General

2/3 · move 2 · cost 4

Unique. A power may have only one General on the board. If yours is killed, you may buy another.

Command. While your General stands in a General post, your Cavalry and Horse Archers in the same wing attack at +1, and so do War Elephants standing in that wing's Cavalry lane. A battle has two wings: the main wing and, in a flanking battle, the flank. A General in the General post commands the Cavalry and Horse Archer lanes, and a General in the Flank General post commands the Flank Cavalry and Flank Horse Archer posts (§10.2).

Protected. A General is never struck while any other unit on its side still stands. Its own hits always land on the enemy army, never on the enemy General (§9.4).

Moves 2 with no penalty. A General may board either kind of ship (1 space), but never fights as a marine.

Attack Boat

3/3 · move 2 · cost 2

A warship. It carries one unit: a heavy infantry unit, who fights from the deck as a marine, or a General.

Transport

1/1 · move 2 · cost 2

Carries 2 spaces of land units: Levies, heavy infantry and Generals take 1 space each, and Cavalry and Siege Engines take 2. A Transport fights badly, but still takes a place in the naval line. Its cargo cannot fight.

Siege Engine

—/— · move 1 · cost 3

Rams, towers and stone-throwers, hauled along with an army.

Breacher. If a Siege Engine is part of an attack on a stronghold, the breach roll gets +2 (§11.2). A second engine adds nothing more.

Behind the lines. A Siege Engine never takes a battle slot, never rolls and is never struck. It is destroyed only when every other unit on its side in the battle is destroyed. It retreats with its army.

Moves 1 and cannot force march. It cannot attack or take a territory on its own, and it does not count toward a garrison. It boards Transports only (2 spaces).

Playtest unit · War Elephant · Carthage and Persia only

Attack 5 · Defense 4 · Move 2 · Cost 7. Not yet in the digital game. Use it only if your group agrees to.

  • Tough. The first hit only wounds an elephant (mark it). A second hit kills it. A wounded elephant fights normally.
  • Rampage. If the die that hits an elephant shows a natural 1, the elephant dies at once, wounded or not. It also tramples: resolve one normal hit against another of its own side's front-line units in the same wing (main or flank). Take the nearest unit in the elephant's own lane (the lower-numbered slot if two are equally near). If there is none, take slot 1 of the wing's other lane, rolling down if it is empty. Units in reserve and Generals are never trampled. A trampled elephant is only wounded, and does not rampage in turn. If no friendly unit can be trampled, the trample does nothing.
  • Flexible. When lining up, choose whether each elephant stands in the Infantry lane or the Cavalry lane. An elephant in a Cavalry lane gets Command from its General, just as Cavalry do.
  • Still undecided: whether elephants can board ships.

7Sequence of Play

The game is played in rounds. In each round, every player takes a turn in the order on the scenario sheet. Then the barbarians act, and the round ends.

7.1Your turn

  1. Collect income. Take gold for every territory you own right now (§3.3). Unspent gold carries over from turn to turn.
  2. Movement stage. Move your units and declare your attacks (§8). Until you announce the Combat stage, you may take back any move or declared attack.
  3. Combat stage. Fight each declared battle, in the order you declared them (§9–12).
  4. Placement stage. Units that did not fight and still have moves left may make non-combat moves. Then buy and place new units (§13). Your turn ends.

7.2End of the round

  1. Barbarian phase. Resolve any scenario event, roll for barbarian spawns, and fight any uprisings (§14).
  2. Check victory (§15). If nobody has won, begin the next round.

8Movement

8.1Steps

A unit moves one step at a time, into an adjacent space. Units move individually, so a stack can split up and send units in different directions. Every unit's moves refresh at the start of its owner's turn.

UnitStepsNotes
Infantry (Levy, heavy infantry)1May force march a second step (§8.2)
Siege Engine1Cannot force march
Cavalry, Horse Archer, General, War Elephant2No penalty
Attack Boat, Transport2Sea zones only

8.2Force march

Infantry may take a second step in the same turn. That step is a force march. If the force-marching step is an attack, the unit attacks at −1 for that battle. Cavalry never take this penalty, even when they travel with force-marching infantry.

8.3Where units can go

  • Your own territory: move in freely.
  • An enemy or barbarian territory with no units in it: take it at once, and flip its control marker. Units with steps left may keep moving.
  • A territory with enemy units in it: this is an attack. Declare it (§8.4). You cannot move through a defended territory. Attacks on a garrisoned stronghold follow §11.
  • A sea zone: see §12.

8.4Declaring attacks

To declare an attack, name the attacking units and the territory they are attacking. The units stay where they are until the Combat stage. Units may move first and attack afterwards. For example, Cavalry can step into your own territory and then attack from there, and infantry can do the same as a force march at −1.

If you attack the same territory from two or more places, it is one battle (§10).

8.5Fighting ends movement

Every unit that fights a battle, on either side, has finished moving for the turn. This includes survivors and units that retreated.

The two sides face each other slot for slot: attacker slot 1 faces defender slot 1, and so on. The Horse Archer lanes are used only by Parthia. Every other power leaves them empty.

9.2Lining up

Both sides start by lining up strongest first: in each lane, the most expensive units take the front slots, with higher Attack breaking ties. Then each side may rearrange its own line. The defender rearranges first, then the attacker.

  • Within a lane, swap freely between front slots and reserve, and set the order of the reserve.
  • A General may move between the General post and the Flank General post, and a Horse Archer between the Horse Archer lane and the Flank Horse Archer post. No other unit may change lanes (War Elephants choose their lane here).
  • Siege Engines take no slot. They wait behind the lines.
  • Barbarians never rearrange. They always line up strongest first.

9.3Fighting a round

  1. Check conditions. Is each infantry line full, with all five front slots occupied? Is each General in its post? These decide this round's bonuses. Losses in this round change nothing until the next round's check (§9.5).
  2. Roll. Both sides roll one d12 for every unit in a front slot, including marines (§12.5). Attackers roll against Attack plus modifiers, and defenders against Defense (plus Phalanx, for Hoplites).
  3. Assign hits (§9.4). All dice count, including those of units killed this round, so combat is simultaneous.
  4. Remove casualties.
  5. Refill. Empty front slots are filled from the same lane's reserve, in reserve order.
  6. Continue or retreat. The attacker may retreat (§9.6). Otherwise fight another round.

9.4Where hits land

  • Straight ahead. A hit rolled from slot 3 strikes the enemy unit in slot 3 of the same lane.
  • Roll down. If that slot is empty, or its unit has already been killed this round, the hit strikes the nearest enemy still standing in that lane. If two are equally near, the hit strikes the lower-numbered slot.
  • Reserves are safe. Units in reserve are never hit. If a lane runs out of standing front-line units partway through a round, any further hits into that lane are lost.
  • Spillover. If an enemy lane was already empty when the round began, hits aimed at it spill into the enemy's first lane that still has units, in this order: Infantry → Cavalry → Flank Infantry → Flank Cavalry → Horse Archer → Flank Horse Archer. The hit strikes slot 1 of that lane (rolling down if slot 1 is empty). This is how cavalry with no cavalry to fight start cutting into the infantry. Horse Archers are reached only after every other lane except the General's has fallen.
  • Generals come last. Hits are never aimed at a General post. They spill into a General only when nothing else on that side is standing. A General's own hits always spill onto the enemy army.

Example round

Rome attacks with 3 Legions, 1 Cavalry and a General. Carthage defends with 5 Heavy Infantry and 1 Cavalry.

The Roman infantry line has 3 of 5 slots filled, so it is not full: the Legions attack at 5 (Manipular). The General is in its post, so the Roman Cavalry attacks at 5. The General itself rolls against 2. Carthage rolls against its Defense: 4 for each Heavy Infantry, 3 for the Cavalry.

The Legion in slot 2 hits, killing the Heavy Infantry in Carthage's slot 2. The General hits too, and its hit spills into Carthage's infantry, slot 1.

Carthage hits from infantry slots 4 and 5. Roman slot 4 is empty, so the first hit rolls down to the nearest standing Legion, in slot 3. The second hit from slot 5 would also reach slot 3 first, but that Legion is already dead, so it rolls on to slot 2.

Rome loses two Legions and Carthage loses two Heavy Infantry. Neither side has infantry in reserve, so the gaps stay open. The Roman line is still not full, so the last Legion keeps its +1 next round.

9.5Modifiers

Modifiers stack. All of them apply to Attack only, except Phalanx, which also applies to Defense. Every other defending unit rolls its plain Defense.

9.6Retreat

Before any round, including the first, the attacking player may call off the assault. All surviving attackers return to the territory the main army attacked from. Landing troops go back aboard their ships instead, and any who no longer fit are lost. The defender keeps the territory.

Only a player who is attacking may retreat. Defenders, barbarians and uprisings (§14.3) never retreat.

9.7Ending a battle

  • Defender destroyed: the attacker wins. Surviving attackers move into the territory and take control of it. The exception is a garrison that sallies out of a stronghold, which stays inside its walls (§11.5).
  • Attacker destroyed: the defender holds.
  • Both destroyed in the same round: the territory stays with the defender, with no units in it.

10Flanking

If you attack one territory from two or more of your territories in the same turn, all of those attackers fight one battle, not several.

10.1Main army and flank

The force with the most units is the main army. Every other force is a flanking detachment. If the two largest forces are tied, the attacker chooses which is the main army. This is decided on the map, when you choose how to split your forces, not on the battle mat.

10.2The flank posts

The main lanes are unchanged: everyone in front fights exactly as usual. Each side also gets flank posts:

Flank InfantryFlank CavalryFlank GeneralFlank Horse Archer
Attacker1111*
Defender—111*
  • The attacker's flank posts are filled only from the flanking detachment. Detachment units that do not fit in a flank post join the main lanes.
  • The defender's Flank Cavalry must be a spare. It comes only from cavalry beyond the one already in the front line, and the defender never pulls its front-line horseman across. Covering a flank costs the defender nothing up front, but it takes a second cavalryman.
  • Generals can flex. An attacking General that marched with the detachment starts in the Flank General post. Either side may move its General between posts while lining up. A General in the flank post gives Command to the Flank Cavalry and Flank Horse Archer.
  • Horse Archers flex too (* Parthia only). They follow the same rules as Generals: an attacking Horse Archer that marched with the detachment starts in the Flank Horse Archer post, and either side may move one between posts while lining up. With both of its Horse Archers, Parthia can put one in each wing. A Parthian flank can be four wide: Flank Infantry, Flank Cavalry, Flank General and Flank Horse Archer.

The attacker's advantage is extra room in the line. The defender has no infantry flank post, so nothing strikes the attacker's Flank Infantry directly. It can only be reached by spillover, once the attacker's main infantry and cavalry have fallen.

11Strongholds & Sieges

A stronghold (§3.5) with a full garrison cannot simply be stormed. First the attacker has to breach its walls, and that can take several turns. Even after the walls fall, the attacker still fights on a narrow front, street by street.

11.1The garrison

A stronghold is fully garrisoned while it holds at least 6 of its owner's land units. Generals and Siege Engines do not count.

  • Fully garrisoned: the attacker must breach the walls before any battle (§11.2).
  • 1 to 5 units: there are too few defenders to man the walls. There is no breach roll: the walls count as breached, and the attacker assaults at once (§11.4).
  • No units: the stronghold is taken like any empty territory (§8.3).

11.2Breaching the walls

When an attack on a fully garrisoned stronghold comes up in the Combat stage, the attacker first makes a breach roll with one d12. On the breach target or lower, the walls are breached, and the assault is fought at once (§11.4).

The breach target starts at 4 and rises by 2 for every breach roll already failed in this siege, as shown by the siege marker. If a Siege Engine is part of the attack, the target is +2 higher.

11.3Laying siege

If the breach roll fails, there is no battle this turn. Instead:

  • Put a siege marker showing 1 on the stronghold, or raise the marker already there by 1.
  • The attacking units stay where they are. They count as having fought (§8.5), so they make no non-combat moves this turn.
  • Arrows from the walls. If the failed roll shows a 12, the defenders shoot down one attacking infantry unit. Line up the attacking infantry strongest first in the four assault slots (§11.4), then roll a d12 to choose the target: 1–3 slot 1, 4–6 slot 2, 7–9 slot 3, 10–12 slot 4. Reroll if you land on an empty slot. That unit takes one hit, so a War Elephant is only wounded. Reserves are never shot. If the attack has no infantry, the arrows miss.

A stronghold with a siege marker on it is besieged, and its breach target rises every turn. Whether the siege also cuts the city off depends on the size of the besieging army:

  • Surrounded. The besieger has at least 6 land units next to the stronghold, in adjacent territories or aboard ships in adjacent sea zones. Generals and Siege Engines do not count. A surrounded city pays no income (§3.3), and its owner may place at most one Levy in it per turn, and nothing else (§13.2).
  • Not surrounded. The besieger has fewer than 6. The breach rolls go on, but the city collects income and raises troops as normal.

Check whether the city is surrounded at the moment it matters: when its owner collects income, and when it places units. Units may still march in and out of a besieged city as normal, so a city that holds out for a few turns can slowly strengthen its garrison.

The siege goes on as long as the besieger attacks the stronghold on every one of its turns. Remove the siege marker, so that any later siege starts again from the first roll, if:

  • the besieger ends a turn without attacking the stronghold
  • the garrison falls below 6 units (the next attack is an assault with no breach roll)
  • the attacker retreats from the assault, or its army is destroyed
  • the stronghold changes hands

11.4Assaulting a stronghold

Once the walls are breached, fight a land battle (§9) inside the city, with these changes:

  • Narrow line. The attacker's infantry lane is 4 slots wide, while the defender keeps all 5. No attacker stands opposite defender slot 5, so hits from that slot roll down to attacker slot 4.
  • The line is never full. A full infantry line needs all five slots (§9.3), and the attacker has only four. Attacking Legions always get Manipular, and attacking Hoplites never get Phalanx. Defending Hoplites get Phalanx (+1 Defense) while the defender's five-slot line is full.
  • No flanking. Attacks from several territories still fight one battle (§10), but neither side has flank posts. Every attacker joins the main lanes.
  • No attacking Horse Archer lane. Parthian Horse Archers assaulting a stronghold fight in the Cavalry lane as ordinary Cavalry. A Parthian garrison keeps its Horse Archer lane.
  • Everything else is unchanged, including the force march and amphibious assault penalties.

11.5Breaking a siege

The defender does not have to wait behind the walls. The garrison, a relief army, or both may attack the besiegers in the territories they are besieging from. This is an ordinary land battle (§9). The defender leaves the walls behind, so it gets no narrow line, and flanking (§10) works as usual.

  • The garrison stays inside the walls. Units that sally out of the stronghold never leave it. Win or lose, survivors remain in the stronghold, and they do not take the besiegers' territory even if they destroy every unit there. A relief army from elsewhere follows the normal rules (§9.7). Units that sallied count as having fought (§8.5).
  • If the besieger is left with fewer than 6 land units next to the stronghold, the city is no longer surrounded.
  • If the besiegers are destroyed, or do not attack the stronghold on their next turn, the siege marker is removed (§11.3).

Example siege

Rome attacks Carthage, a stronghold held by 5 Heavy Infantry and 1 Cavalry. The Roman army has 6 Legions, 1 Cavalry and a General.

Turn 1. Rome needs a 4 or lower and rolls 9. Carthage gets siege marker 1. The six Legions stay in their territory next door, which is enough to surround the city, so Carthage pays no income. On its own turn, Carthage buys a Levy in the city (the most a surrounded stronghold may place) and marches in a Heavy Infantry from a neighbouring territory. The garrison is now 8.

Turn 2. A Siege Engine that Rome brought up in its last Placement stage joins the attack. The target is 6, plus 2 for the engine, so 8. Rome rolls 7, and the walls are breached.

The assault. Four Legions fill the narrow line and two wait in reserve. A line of four is never full, so every Legion attacks at 5 (Manipular) from the first round. Carthage answers with five Heavy Infantry in its front line, rolling against their Defense of 4, with the Levy and the extra Heavy Infantry in reserve.

12War at Sea

12.1Ships

Ships are built in sea zones and never go ashore. They move 2 steps between adjacent sea zones.

12.2Boarding

A land unit boards a ship by stepping from a coastal territory into an adjacent sea zone where you have a ship with room. Boarding uses one step. Units aboard move with their ship.

ShipSpaceMay carry
Attack Boat1One heavy infantry unit (as a marine) or one General
Transport2Levy, heavy infantry, General (1 space each); Cavalry, Siege Engine (2 spaces)

When heavy infantry board and both kinds of ship have room, the owner chooses. On an Attack Boat they fight as marines, but in the front of the line. On a Transport they are safer cargo that cannot fight.

12.3Landing

Units aboard ship land by stepping onto an adjacent land territory. Landing counts as a step, and the ships stay at sea.

  • Your territory, or one with no units in it: the troops land, and take the territory if it was an enemy's.
  • A defended territory: this is an amphibious assault, and the landing troops attack at −1. If they retreat or are beaten back, survivors return to the ships if there is room. Any who do not fit are lost.

12.4Naval battles

Moving ships into a sea zone that holds enemy ships is an attack, and is declared and fought like a land battle. At sea there is only one lane: the naval line, 4 slots wide, shared by Attack Boats and Transports.

Lining up strongest first puts Attack Boats in front of Transports. A convoy without enough escorts to fill the line has to put Transports in it, and pays for that in lost cargo. With only one lane, hits never spill at sea. All other battle rules — rounds, roll-down, retreat — are the same as on land.

12.5Marines

Heavy infantry aboard an Attack Boat fight from its deck. A marine rolls its own die, with its own Attack or Defense, from the boat's slot. A hit on that slot kills the marine first, and the next hit sinks the boat. A loaded Attack Boat therefore rolls two dice and takes two hits to clear. It adds fighting strength without taking an extra slot.

12.6Sinking

When a ship sinks, everything aboard goes down with it.

13Raising Troops

13.1Non-combat moves

At the start of the Placement stage, any unit that did not fight this turn and still has steps left may move. It can go into your own territories or empty ones, board ships, sail, and land on friendly or empty shores. No attacks are allowed in this stage, and that includes sailing into enemy ships.

13.2Buying

  • Spend gold on units from your power's roster (§6).
  • Land units are placed in any territory you owned at the start of your turn and still own. Ground you took this turn cannot raise troops yet. A surrounded stronghold can take at most one Levy per turn (§11.3).
  • Ships are placed in any sea zone adjacent to such a territory.
  • You may have only one General on the board (§5).
  • New units cannot move until your next turn.
  • Unspent gold is kept for later turns.

14Barbarians

Barbarians are a non-player power. They hold territory at the start of the game and defend it. They collect no income, buy nothing, and never retreat. Unless a scenario says otherwise, they never attack.

14.1Spawn provinces

The scenario sheet lists the spawn provinces, usually barbarian homelands at the edges of the map. In each Barbarian phase, roll a d12 for every spawn province. On a success, one barbarian Levy appears there.

14.2Unrest and pacification

The spawn province is…Levy onChance
Held by barbarians1–433%
Occupied by you, with heavy infantry there (pacified)1–433%
Occupied by you, without heavy infantry (unrest)1–867%

Only heavy infantry (Heavy Infantry, Legion or Hoplite) keep a province calm. Levies and Cavalry do not. Occupying a province stirs up unrest, and holding it quietly takes a real garrison.

14.3Uprisings

If a Levy appears in a province you occupy:

  • You have units there: fight a battle immediately. Your army defends, the barbarians attack, and neither side may retreat.
  • You have no units there: the province goes back to the barbarians.

14.4Rolling for the barbarians

Any player who is not in the battle rolls the barbarians' dice. Barbarians always line up strongest first and never rearrange.

14.5Scenario events

Some scenarios add more to the Barbarian phase, such as a random event every round, barbarian invasions across unguarded frontiers, or a named enemy that roams the map. Those rules are on the scenario sheet. Resolve any event first, then the spawn rolls, then any invasions.

15Victory & Elimination

15.1Winning

The scenario sheet says how the game is won. Victory is checked at the end of every round, after the Barbarian phase. Common types include:

  • Hold the empire. Survive to a set round while keeping your starting territory. Results are often graded by how much you still hold.
  • Take and keep. Capture an enemy capital and still hold it at the end of a set number of rounds in a row. If you lose it at any point, the count resets to zero.
  • Last power standing. In any game with rivals, a player who outlasts every other player wins.

15.2Elimination

A power is destroyed when it owns no territory and has no units anywhere on the board. An army still in the field, or at sea, keeps its power alive. A destroyed power's turns are skipped for the rest of the game.

Sieges

  • Garrison of 6+ land units (not Generals or engines) → breach roll first; fewer → assault at once
  • Breach on 4, then 6, 8, 10, automatic · Siege Engine +2
  • Failed roll → raise siege marker; besiegers stay put and count as having fought
  • Failed roll of 12 → one random front-slot infantry unit takes a hit
  • Surrounded (besieger has 6+ land units adjacent): no income; at most 1 Levy per turn

  • Garrison or relief army may attack the besiegers: a normal battle, no narrow line. Garrison survivors stay inside the walls.
